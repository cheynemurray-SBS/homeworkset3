1Confidence intervalsE-commerce delivery performance20 pts

You’re the operations manager for an e-commerce company that promises delivery within 3 days on average. To check whether you’re meeting that promise, you randomly sample 45 recent orders and record delivery times in days.

delivery_times = [
    2.8, 3.2, 2.9, 3.5, 2.7, 3.1, 3.4, 2.6, 3.3, 3.0,

    3.5, 2.8, 3.1, 2.9, 3.2, 3.0, 3.4, 2.7, 3.3, 3.1,
    2.9, 3.2, 3.0, 3.5, 2.8, 3.1, 3.3, 2.9, 3.2, 3.0,
    3.4, 2.8, 3.1, 3.3, 2.9
]

1.1 Calculate and visualize 10 pts

  1. Calculate in Python (3 pts): sample mean and standard deviation, and the 95% confidence interval for the true mean delivery time. Show every component: degrees of freedom, critical t-value, standard error, and margin of error.
  2. Visualize (4 pts): a professional chart showing the sample mean clearly marked, the 95% interval as error bars, the 3-day promise as a reference line, and proper labels, title, and legend.
  3. Interpret (3 pts): explain the interval in plain language for a non-technical executive.

1.2 Business decision and trade-offs 10 pts

  1. (3 pts) Based on your interval, is there evidence the company is failing its 3-day promise? Explain using the interval.
  2. (3 pts) Your boss says, “The average is 3.05 days, so we’re basically meeting our promise. Close enough.” Respond in three or four sentences using statistical reasoning.
  3. (4 pts) Calculate the 95% interval using only the first 20 observations and compare it to the n = 45 interval. Explain to your boss why sample size matters and whether collecting more data would be worthwhile.

2.1 Conduct a complete hypothesis test at α = 0.05 12 pts

  1. Hypotheses and test choice (3 pts): state H₀ and Hₐ, explain whether the test is one- or two-tailed and why, and state your significance level.
  2. Assumption checks (3 pts): a histogram or Q-Q plot to assess normality, a comment on whether assumptions are reasonably met, and a brief note on independence.
  3. Test execution (3 pts): use scipy.stats.ttest_1samp() for the test statistic and p-value, show code and output, and calculate the 95% interval for the true mean.
  4. Statistical conclusion (3 pts): reject or fail to reject H₀, explain what it means for the business without jargon, and report both the p-value and the interval.

2.2 Business impact analysis 8 pts

  1. Type I and Type II errors (4 pts): describe what each error would mean here and its business consequence, then say which is worse for your company and why.
  2. Statistical vs. practical significance (4 pts): using the figures below, calculate whether the observed increase is practically significant and show your work. Then recommend whether to continue the campaign, using both statistical and business reasoning.
    • Campaign cost to develop and implement: $15,000
    • Revenue per additional email opened: $2.50 on average
    • Customers in the database: 50,000

Tips for success

Every analysis should connect to a real business decision. Don’t just report statistics; explain what they mean and what should be done.

State hypotheses firstWrite H₀ and Hₐ before you run any test.
Check assumptionsDon’t skip it. A plot and a sentence are enough to show you looked.
Two kinds of significanceA result can be statistically significant and still not worth the money. Address both.
Write for the audienceYour reader is a business professional, not a statistician. Be honest about limitations and make a clear recommendation.
Readable codeUse meaningful variable names, comment your thinking, and print intermediate results to verify them.
Visuals matterBudget real time for them. A chart that management can read at a glance earns its points.
Show your workInclude code, outputs, and explanations. Partial credit depends on seeing your thinking.
Portfolio qualityTreat this as something you’d be proud to show a future employer.
